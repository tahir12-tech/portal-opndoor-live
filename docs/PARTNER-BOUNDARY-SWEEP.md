# Where `partner_id` is treated as a company boundary

An audit of every site that bounds data by partner, and whether an agency user
reaches another agency through it on the shared `opndoor-agents` house partner.

**Why the class exists.** For a SUPPLIER, one partner is one company and
`partner_id = app_partner()` is a real boundary. On the AGENCY rail every
independently onboarded agency shares one house partner, so the same expression
means "every agency Opndoor has onboarded". On dev that partner carries four
unrelated, competing agencies (Harborview, Northgate, Regent's, Southbank) and
six management users between them.

**The precondition matters.** Most of the read leaks below need the caller to be
UNPOSITIONED. A positioned agency user is narrowed by `app_has_scope()` /
`app_scope_branches()`; the usual shape falls back to the whole partner only when
somebody holds no `user_scopes` row. Measured on dev with the old policies
restored: Rosa positioned saw 7 applications in 1 agency and wrote to 0 of
Northgate's 14; with her position removed she saw 24 across 3 agencies and wrote
to 14 of 14. Today no management user on the house partner is unpositioned, and
`invite-user` can create one (`if (scopeKind)` is conditional), so the exposure is
latent rather than active.

**Totals.** 272 sites examined: **140 leak**, 31 depend on the caller,
101 are correct as they stand. 16 of the leaking sites are
fixed in this batch; the rest are listed as outstanding.

**The shape of the fix, everywhere.** `app_scoped_agencies()` resolves a user's
positions (branch, agency, group) and an unpositioned negotiator's home branch,
and has NO partner-wide fallback. `agencies`, `branches`, `agent_contacts` and
`agency_groups` were converted to it in 20260924120000 and are clean. Everything
below should move to the same helper rather than grow a second rule.
